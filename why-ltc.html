Why LTC?

Most families will face this. Very few are ready.

Long-term care isn't a rare event. It's a common, expensive part of aging - and the bill usually lands on you.

How care begins

It rarely starts with a nursing home.

The need starts when everyday activities stop being safe to manage alone.

  • Aging and physical decline

    Bathing, dressing, and moving safely get harder as strength and mobility fade.

  • A sudden injury

    One fall can mean months of rehab and hands-on help for someone fully independent the week before.

  • Progressive illness

    Stroke, Parkinson's, or heart disease can create a need for help measured in years.

  • Cognitive impairment

    Dementia often calls for supervision long before there's any physical limitation.

The family impact

Are you prepared to care for the people you love?

When there's no plan, someone in the family becomes the plan. Most care is given by a spouse or an adult child - unpaid, untrained, and usually while holding down a job.

It's worth asking now, while the choice is still yours: who would step in, for how long, and what would it cost them?

Medicare

Only covers up to 100 days.

And only after a qualifying hospital stay. After day 100, the cost is yours.

Source: Medicare.gov, "Skilled Nursing Facility (SNF) Care" coverage guidelines - Medicare covers up to 100 days per benefit period after a qualifying 3-day inpatient hospital stay, with coinsurance after day 20.

Medicaid

Coverage after you've spent down.

You generally have to drain your savings to about $2,000 before Medicaid kicks in. Then choice narrows.

Source: Medicaid.gov eligibility rules and KFF, "10 Things About Long-Term Services and Supports (LTSS)," 2024/2025 - most states cap countable assets near $2,000 for an individual applicant.
