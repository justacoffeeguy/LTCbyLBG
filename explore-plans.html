One risk. Three ways to plan.

Each type of long-term care plan solves the same problem in a different way.

Same problem. Very different ways to solve it.

Traditional long-term care insurance is built specifically for care. LTC + life insurance combines long-term care benefits with a life insurance death benefit. LTC + annuities use an annuity structure with long-term care protection. Each can provide meaningful protection, but they differ in underwriting, premium structure, what happens if care is never needed, and how benefits are paid.

Three Options

Three approaches - depending on what matters most to you.

Some people want the most dedicated care coverage they can get. Some want protection without feeling like unused dollars simply disappear. Others would rather reposition an existing asset and keep whatever value is not used for care. Those different priorities point toward three different ways to plan.

01

Traditional LTC Insurance

A stand-alone policy designed specifically to reimburse qualifying long-term care expenses, up to the benefit limits selected when the policy is issued.

Best fit to explore when: maximizing dedicated long-term care protection is the priority.
02

LTC + Life Insurance

A life insurance policy with long-term care protection. If care is needed, benefits can help pay for it. If the LTC benefit does not exhaust the policy, a death benefit will remain for beneficiaries.

Best fit to explore when: you want care protection and a legacy/death-benefit component.
03

LTC + Annuities

An annuity structure with long-term care protection. The annuity value can fund qualifying care, and if care does not exhaust it, remaining value stays available for you and can ultimately pass to beneficiaries.

Best fit to explore when: you have assets to reposition and want less stringent underwriting than many other LTC solutions require.
01

Traditional Long-Term Care Insurance

The most direct approach: buy a policy whose primary job is paying for long-term care.

Traditional LTC insurance is designed to reimburse covered long-term care services up to a pre-selected daily or monthly limit. Coverage can be structured for care at home, in community settings, assisted living, or a nursing facility, depending on the policy.

Because the policy is dedicated to long-term care, the premium is devoted to care protection rather than also supporting a separate death benefit or annuity value. Health underwriting is required before a traditional policy can be issued.

Basic structureUse it or lose it

A basic traditional policy is built for care and generally does not return unused premium or provide a death benefit.

State Partnership protectionQualifying policies can protect assets

Qualifying traditional policies may participate in a state Partnership Program that can protect a portion of assets after LTC benefits are exhausted.

Where the premium goesFocused on care

The premium supports long-term care coverage rather than also funding a separate death benefit or annuity value.

What triggers benefits?

Policies generally require the insured to meet the contract's definition of chronically ill, commonly involving an inability to perform at least two activities of daily living or a qualifying cognitive impairment, with required certification.

What happens if care is never needed?

With a basic traditional policy, it is generally use it or lose it - there is no death benefit and unused premium is not returned. Optional return-of-premium or nonforfeiture features can change that outcome.

Can premiums change?

Traditional policy premiums are not guaranteed and can increase in the future. Some limited-pay structures can reduce or eliminate that exposure after the required payment period.

How are claims paid?

Many traditional LTC policies reimburse covered expenses up to the policy limit. Some policy designs instead pay a set benefit directly under the contract. The payment method matters because it affects documentation and flexibility.

02

LTC + Life Insurance

One pool of premium dollars designed to address two possible outcomes: care during life or a benefit at death.

LTC + life insurance combines a life insurance contract with long-term care protection. If a qualifying long-term care need occurs, the policy can provide LTC benefits. If those benefits do not exhaust the policy, a death benefit will remain for beneficiaries.

This structure can reduce the use-it-or-lose-it concern of basic traditional LTC insurance. The tradeoff is that the premium is funding both long-term care protection and a life insurance benefit, so the economics are different from a policy built only for care.

If care is neededLTC benefits are available

Benefits can help pay for qualifying long-term care needs under the contract.

If care is not exhaustedTax-free death benefit

If LTC benefits do not exhaust the policy, a death benefit will remain for beneficiaries. If properly structured, life insurance proceeds are generally income tax-free to the beneficiary.

Premium structureUsually guaranteed

The brochure notes asset-based life premiums are usually guaranteed, so future rate increases generally are not the main concern.

Underwriting

Asset-based life policies still require underwriting, but the brochure describes the process as simplified health underwriting for this category. Product and carrier rules vary.

What remains for beneficiaries?

Long-term care benefits generally reduce the amount ultimately left as a death benefit. If the LTC benefit does not exhaust the policy, the remaining death benefit will pass to beneficiaries subject to the policy terms.

LTC rider vs. chronic illness rider

They are not the same. An LTC rider is described as more comprehensive, while chronic illness riders may require a condition to be permanent or non-recoverable and may not cover every LTC claim.

How benefits may be paid

Some LTC riders reimburse qualifying expenses up to the policy limit. Others can pay a set benefit directly under the contract without tying every dollar to a submitted expense.

Effect on the death benefit

When long-term care benefits are accelerated from a life insurance policy, using those benefits generally reduces what ultimately remains as a death benefit.

Flexibility of benefit use

Certain life-based riders may allow benefit dollars to help with a broad set of needs such as family/home care, remodeling, transportation, housekeeping, assisted living, or skilled nursing, subject to the contract.

03

LTC + Annuities

An annuity structure with long-term care protection.

LTC + annuities combine an annuity structure with long-term care protection. The annuity value can fund qualifying LTC expenses up to the policy's specified monthly amount, and an additional extension-of-benefits pool may be purchased to provide coverage after the annuity value has been depleted.

This route may be especially relevant for someone who has assets to reposition. If the annuity is not spent entirely on care, the remaining annuity value stays available to the owner and can ultimately remain for beneficiaries.

UnderwritingOften less stringent

The brochure describes underwriting as less stringent than traditional LTC insurance or life insurance.

If care does not exhaust itRemaining value stays yours

Any annuity value not spent on care remains available to the owner and can ultimately pass to beneficiaries.

Qualified LTC expensesPotential tax advantage

The brochure states that qualified LTC payments from an asset-based annuity can be income-tax-free under the Pension Protection Act.

Where benefits come from first

The contract initially pays LTC expenses from the annuity's own value, up to the specified maximum monthly amount.

Extension of benefits

An additional LTC pool may be purchased so benefits can continue after the annuity value is exhausted. The brochure notes extended-benefit premiums are typically guaranteed.

What happens to the annuity value?

As long-term care benefits are paid, the annuity value is being spent down. If care does not exhaust the annuity, whatever remains stays available to the owner and can ultimately pass to beneficiaries. Other withdrawals can also reduce LTC benefits and any death benefit.

Not every annuity “LTC feature” is the same

An annuity income doubler is a separate concept: it may temporarily increase the income payout when contract conditions are met, but it is not the same structure as a true asset-based LTC annuity with an extension-of-benefits pool.

Care setting rules vary

Some annuity riders are limited to nursing-home confinement or terminal illness, while others may recognize qualifying home care. Contract definitions and benefit periods need to be reviewed carefully.

Interest and opportunity cost

The brochure notes that the cost of LTC coverage can consume some or all of the interest earned by the annuity, particularly in a low-interest-rate environment.
