Why Care?

Long-term care isn't about age. It's about ability.

Care isn't triggered by a birthday — it's triggered by a specific, defined event.

What actually triggers a long-term care need

A long-term care "triggering event" occurs when a person cannot perform at least two of six Activities of Daily Living (ADLs) over an extended period of time, and this is certified by a physician or nurse practitioner:

Bathing Dressing Toileting Transferring Continence Eating

Care can also be triggered by cognitive impairment alone — memory loss, poor judgment, disorientation, impaired reasoning, or a reduced ability to make decisions — even without any physical limitation.

Who actually needs long-term care?

Physical decline due to aging

The most common path. As strength and mobility decline with age, everyday tasks like bathing, dressing, and moving safely become difficult without help.

Debilitating injury

A fall or accident can require months of rehab and hands-on help with basic activities of daily living, even for someone who was fully independent before.

Progressive illness

Conditions like stroke or Parkinson's can bring on paralysis or reduced motor function, often requiring sustained assistance over years, not weeks.

Severe cognitive impairment

Dementia and related conditions bring memory loss, poor judgment, and disorientation. Coverage can trigger here without any physical limitation at all.

Combination of chronic conditions

Many people don't face one single event, but a slow accumulation of chronic conditions that together require regular, ongoing support.

Know your risk

These aren't fringe scenarios. The numbers show that planning for long-term care is closer to the norm than the exception.

It's not only a financial risk
