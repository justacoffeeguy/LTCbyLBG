About Les

More than 40 years helping people make confident financial decisions.

Les brings decades of experience across investment management, wealth planning, retirement planning, asset protection, estate planning strategies, and long-term care. Today, he applies that broader financial perspective exclusively to helping individuals and couples prepare for long-term care.

Retirement is not just about building wealth. It is about protecting the life you have worked so hard to create.

For more than 40 years, I have helped individuals and couples make informed financial decisions with confidence. Before founding Personal Financial Strategies, Inc. in 1996, I served as a Vice President at a major Wall Street investment firm, where I gained firsthand experience in investment management and wealth planning. That experience reinforced an important lesson: accumulating wealth is only part of the equation; protecting it is just as important.

During my career, I spent more than 25 years helping clients grow, preserve, and transfer their wealth through comprehensive financial planning. My work centered on asset protection, estate planning strategies, retirement planning, and long-term care planning, always with the goal of helping clients protect what they had worked so hard to build.

I retired from Personal Financial Strategies in 2022, leaving the firm in capable hands. Soon afterward, I chose to return to the financial-planning profession with one exclusive focus: helping individuals and couples prepare for the possibility of long-term care. I enjoy working, but more importantly, I value the opportunity to help people make decisions that can profoundly affect their lives and their families. I believe this work is as important as any business role I have ever undertaken.

Why? Because one unexpected health event can jeopardize a lifetime of careful planning. Long-term care is not simply an insurance decision; it is a family decision. It can affect your financial security, your independence, your choices, and the people you love most. Helping individuals and couples prepare for that possibility is both a privilege and a responsibility that I take very seriously.

My passion for educating others also led me to write The Big Picture Retirement Planning: From 30,000 Feet, a book designed to simplify the often-complex world of retirement planning. Throughout my career, I have believed that informed clients make better decisions. My role is to educate first, listen carefully, and help you understand your options so you can move forward with confidence.

As an independent long-term care insurance advisor, my loyalty is to my clients, not to any insurance company. If you do not need coverage, if your existing policy remains your best option, or if purchasing coverage would not be financially appropriate, I will tell you directly. When coverage does make sense, I compare solutions from leading insurance carriers and recommend a plan based on your health, financial goals, family circumstances, and budget.

My commitment is not to sell a particular policy. It is to help you make the right decision for your future. That client-first philosophy has guided me throughout my career, and it remains the foundation of my work today.

Planning ahead is not about expecting the worst. It is about protecting your independence, preserving your retirement savings, reducing the burden on your loved ones, and gaining the confidence that comes from being prepared.

I look forward to helping you do exactly that.

How I approach these conversations

Decades of financial-planning experience shape the way I look at long-term care. The policy is only one piece of a much larger financial and family picture.

Education first

Understand before you decide

Before we talk about any product, we talk about what long-term care actually is, what triggers it, and what it could cost - so any decision you make is one you fully understand.

No forced fit

Your situation drives the plan

Traditional coverage, LTC + life insurance, or LTC + annuities can each make sense for different reasons. Your health, assets, goals, family circumstances, and budget should drive the decision.

Honest, either way

Sometimes the answer is "you're fine"
